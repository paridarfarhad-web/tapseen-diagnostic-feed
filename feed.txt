What if they were already here, quietly changing the world around us while the rest of humanity went about its business? That's a considerably creepier idea, and one forgotten science fiction movie had a lot of fun exploring it.

Released in 1996, this Charlie Sheen thriller took a different approach to the usual extraterrestrial invasion story. Instead of giving us another Independence Day-style battle for the planet, it imagined something much more insidious. The aliens weren't necessarily interested in blowing up our cities. They wanted to take over without attracting attention. Thirty years later, the movie's central idea still makes for a fascinating piece of science fiction . And there's a pretty good reason it deserves another look.

The Arrival follows Zane Zaminsky, an astronomer played by Charlie Sheen who discovers a strange signal coming from deep space. Naturally, he does what any curious scientist would do. He digs deeper into things. Unfortunately, his discovery puts him on the radar of some very dangerous people. Next thing he knows, Zane finds himself involved in a conspiracy that stretches much further than he could have imagined. The really interesting part is what he discovers. The aliens aren't waiting for humanity to notice them. They've already established themselves on Earth, and they've been working on something much bigger than a simple visit.

That's what makes the movie's invasion so unsettling. Director David Twohy doesn't rely on enormous spacecraft hovering over New York or armies of extraterrestrials marching through the streets. Instead, he builds the story around the possibility that the people running parts of our world might not be human at all. It's a wonderfully paranoid premise, especially when Zane starts realizing how difficult it is to convince anybody that something extraordinary is happening. Sheen makes for an entertaining lead because Zane isn't some unstoppable action hero. He's a scientist who stumbles into something terrifying and has to figure out what to do with the information. There's a little bit of The X-Files in the whole affair, which is hardly a bad thing for a 1990s sci-fi thriller.

The Arrival really knew how to get the most mileage out of its relatively simple premise. The movie understands that an alien invasion doesn't necessarily need to involve the destruction of famous landmarks to be frightening. Sometimes the idea that something is happening right under our noses is considerably worse. The aliens have their own agenda, and humanity isn't exactly in a position to negotiate. Twohy also gives the film some memorable science fiction imagery, particularly as Zane gets closer to understanding what the extraterrestrials are doing. The story gets increasingly strange, but it never loses sight of its central mystery. That's an important distinction. Plenty of sci-fi movies have interesting ideas that fall apart once the explanations start arriving. The Arrival manages to keep things entertaining.

The movie also arrived during a particularly enjoyable period for science fiction . The 1990s gave moviegoers everything from Independence Day and Stargate to Contact and The X-Files . But there was also room for smaller stories that approached the subject from a different angle. The Arrival landed somewhere between those two worlds. It had enough action and spectacle to keep things moving, but its real appeal came from the mystery surrounding the invasion. I wouldn't necessarily put it alongside the genre's undisputed classics, but that's not really the point. It's an entertaining little sci-fi thriller with an idea that deserved more attention than it received. And if you've spent the last few decades watching aliens announce their arrival by blowing up the White House, seeing one quietly take over the planet is a refreshing change of pace.
