The Marvel Comics parade float was sponsored by the publisher to promote its comic books, toys, and merchandise IP - long before the brand would venture into animated TV series and over two decades before the MCU would debut with Iron Man in 2008. Forty years later, the float is still a fun peice of 1980s nostalgia but, within the context of Doomsday set to arrive in mere weeks, it's also a fun preview of the two characters at the dead center of the Avengers mega-movie: Steve Rogers and Doctor Doom .

Check out a full clip of the float and its "story" below:

The clip includes a brief two minute "story" that sees Doctor Strange summon Captain America from the pages of a Marvel comic book to help Wolverine who is being attacked by Lady Loki and Magneto - only to confront Doctor Doom who has imprisoned Power Man (aka Luke Cage). Captain America, along with the help from RoboCop (we will get to that), frees Luke Cage and heads to take on more of the assembled bad guys - including Emma Frost and The Hulk (who is up to his "old tricks again"). The skit concludes with The Hulk toppling a clock tower before being incapacitated by Cap. Daredevil, Iron Man, Green Goblin, and more also appear. The entire thing is set to the iconic Back to the Future theme.

As for why RoboCop was on a Marvel Comics float in 1987, the original film that featured Alex Murphy's transformation into RoboCop had debuted in July of the same year - and went on to become a pop culture phenomenon; however, more importantly, Marvel Comics held a license to the character for comic books and other projects (Marvel actually produced the RoboCop animated series in 1988), so his appearance - while jarring from a first-party Marvel IP stand point, was actually a clever piece of cross-promotional branding that highlighted Marvel's reach in pop culture (even outside of their own characters).

The iconic Spider-Man balloon debuted the same year and, unlike the float, actually continued to make an appearance in the parade until bowing out in 1998. An updated Spider-Man balloon would return in 2009 and appear until returning in 2014 - a 5-year run that was not without controversy. In 2013, the Spider-Man balloon snagged on a tree - which ripped a large hole that caused the left arm to deflate.

There is no Marvel activation planned for the 2026 Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade.

With a character like the Joker, his origins can range from the tragic to the terrifying. Almost everyone tries, in some form, to explain how a monster like the Joker could have been created and how he started his eternal rivalry with Batman .

But the worst revelation was that Aunt Eunice would regularly brush her nephew down with bleach. Naturally, this treatment would eventually lead to the Joker’s skin turning pure white. Aunt Eunice would also constantly gloat about how she brutally tortured and murdered her late husband to make her nephew even more terrified of her. Unlike other Joker origins that involve one terrible accident, his backstory in this issue shows how he was molded by the twisted woman who raised, abused, and disfigured him for years.

However, Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars was unlike anything Marvel or DC had ever attempted in the past. This was the first major site-wide crossover series, and it changed everything about how comic books were made from that point on.

Even before Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars #1 (1984) by Jim Shooter and Michael Zeck came out, Marvel was already shaking up the entire brand in the most innovative way possible.

I was a kid when Marvel Comics prepared to start its Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars comic book series, and I had no idea what was going on. For most of my reading life, I was following mostly single and small multi-issue stories, and even when comic books crossed over, it was a big deal. When Marvel Super Heroes Contest of Champions came out in 1982, it was incredible, especially for a kid who had never dreamed he would see so many heroes fighting in one series.

What Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars did was take that idea and blow up the entire Marvel Universe. That is because Marvel showed before the series was even underway that it was going to change everything in Marvel and reset several major titles. This all started on the last page of most major comics published in January 1984, where the heroes went to Central Park and ended up disappearing without a trace. What makes this so amazing is that the results of Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars were shown before the miniseries even hit its second issue. That is because all the heroes were back the next month, and there were major changes that happened without any explanation. First up, Spider-Man returned with a new black costume in The Amazing Spider-Man #252 , and Spider-Man could put it on and take it off by just commanding it with his mind. It took seven months before Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars #8 came out and showed him getting the costume, and it took even longer to prove this was a symbiote.

The Fantastic Four was back, but The Thing was no longer with them. In fact, the solo The Thing series had him on a barren planet having adventures while the Fantastic Four introduced the world to their newest team member, She-Hulk. It wasn't until Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars ended that The Thing's departure was explained. Colossus and Kitty Pryde's relationship was destroyed when he told Kitty about his relationship with Zsaji on Battleworld, which was a good thing thanks to their age difference. The miniseries took a while to show what Colossus did and who Zsaji even was.

Magneto came back with a new attitude, and Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars showed why he started his change from a villain to an antihero and eventually an actual X-Men member. The Hulk returned with an injured leg, and his intelligence was lessened a great deal, returning him more in line with his Savage Hulk persona. It was a huge reset for Magneto and the Hulk, and once again, it took several months to show what caused the changes in both men. Nothing like this had ever happened before in comics, and it changed everything in Marvel and DC.

In Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars , a cosmic entity known as the Beyonder captured heroes and villains from Earth and forced them to fight each other. This was all based on the Beyonder's curiosity to understand humans. He offered the winning side anything they wished for, which was very enticing for the villains. By the end, everyone was back home except for The Thing, and nothing was the same.

In just two years, Marvel and DC each had a series that threw everyone into one comic book storyline and then shook up the entire universe when it was done.

In today's comic book world, there are often multiple site-wide crossover events every year. In 2026 alone, there have been "Avengers: Armageddon," "Queen in Black," and the upcoming "DNX." Titles like "Civil War," "Annihilation," "Secret Empire," and "House of M" wouldn't have existed without Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars . The 2015 "Secret Wars" event only exists because of this earlier series . Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars not only blew the mind of a kid who loved comics, but also changed how the major companies published their series.
