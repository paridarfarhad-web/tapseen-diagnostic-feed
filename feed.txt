Still, Cumberbatch's best performance of his career came with his role as Sherlock Holmes in the BBC Sherlock series.

While Benedict Cumberbatch's role as Doctor Strange was seen by more people than anything else he has ever done, thanks to the Avengers movies and his Spider-Man appearance, his role as Sherlock Holmes is better all around , at least for his character. Sherlock debuted in 2010 from creators Mark Gatiss and Steven Moffat, and it stars Cumberbatch as the legendary detective, Sherlock Holmes, and Martin Freeman as Doctor John Watson. Both characters are based on the literary figures created by Sir Arthur Conan Doyle.

Airing on the BBC, the show was unique, especially for American audiences. There were four seasons, but each season had only three 90-minute episodes, each telling a different Sherlock Holmes mystery story. Through the four seasons (and one additional special), some of the most iconic Sherlock Holmes stories were adapted, including A Study in Scarlet (renamed &#8220;A Study in Pink&#8221;) in Season 1, The Hound of the Baskervilles (renamed &#8220;The Hounds of Baskerville&#8221;) in Season 2, &#8220;The Adventure of Charles Augustus Milverton&#8221; (renamed &#8220;His Last Vow&#8221;) in Season 3, and &#8220;The Adventure of the Final Problem&#8221; (renamed &#8220;The Final Problem&#8221;) in Season 4. There have been countless adaptations of Sherlock Holmes over the years, with names like Basil Rathbone, Peter Cushing, Jeremy Brett, and Robert Downey Jr. taking on the role, but Benedict Cumberbatch matches up to all of them. Not only that, but the BBC Sherlock series is one of the best adaptations on TV or in the movies based on its quality of storytelling and the look of the series. It doesn't get much better than this, and Cumberbatch is a big reason why.

What really sets this series apart is that it removes Sherlock Holmes from the era most of the previous adaptations used, and instead places him in present-day London. This allows the series to really delve into who Sherlock Holmes is as a character rather than just have him as the typical detective genius he is usually portrayed as. Moffat and Gatiss also do a great job framing the scenes, so the audience sees the clues as Sherlock sees them, and the editing shows how he finds them, making Cumberbatch's performance even more impressive. His dry wit, precision, and awe are inspiring, and his performance did more to make Sherlock Holmes a real person than anything before, including the Doyle stories.

There have been rumors of another Sherlock season since the fourth ended. There are plenty of stories that could still be told, but the fact that Cumberbatch has become such an in-demand actor and Freeman continues to get plenty of work makes the timing of actually doing another 270 minutes of stories hard to line up based on their schedules. While Moffat has said a fifth series was plotted by himself and Gatiss, it never happened.

Cumberbatch himself said he would like to do it, but Moffat said that Cumberbatch's movie career was not going to make it easy. Freeman even said never to close the door on another season, and he said he was interested in returning if the material was of good quality. However, things seem to have changed, as Gatiss said in 2025 that Cumberbatch and Freeman had decided they were no longer interested in returning for a new season, seeming to move on from the material and push forward with their careers. Interestingly enough, Robert Downey Jr. might have another Sherlock Holmes movie coming more than a decade after the last film was released, but it is Benedict Cumberbatch's version that really deserves another episode.

The Jetsons Meet the Flintstones came out on November 15, 1987, and it was released on VHS four times from 1988 up to 2001. It was also released on DVD in June 2011. The film followed fan-favorite characters from both franchises, but, despite advertising for the film, it didn’t include The Flintstones characters Orbitty, Pebbles, and Bamm-Bamm. The Jetsons Meet the Flintstones also marked a return to previous form, with the film being the first Jetsons project to use traditional cels for animation since “The Vacation” in The Jetsons.

Even 39 years later, the film is still a little mind-blowing, even while it makes perfect sense. The Jetsons and The Flintstones were both cartoons centered around families navigating a world unfamiliar to viewers. While The Flintstones has had a film since , The Jetsons hasn’t, so perhaps it’s the combination of the two that The Jetsons needs to work for a film format. A Crossover Made Possible by Time Travel A crossover between The Flintstones and The Jetsons was always going to involve time travel. Either it would be time travel putting them into each other’s path, or the creators would have to reveal that they weren’t as far apart in time as they first appeared. The main question to answer then was if the Flintstones were going forward in time, or if the Jetsons were going to go back. Over the course of the film’s ninety-two-minute runtime, the answer would be both.

At first, the Jetsons go back in time. Elroy Jetson manages to create a time travel machine, and the Jetson family decides they are due for a relaxing vacation. The time travel machine takes them to the camping park where the Flintstones have decided to take a holiday of their own. The two families become friends with the Jetsons sharing their futuristic gadgets with the Flintstones. In hopes of getting his job back — Fred lost it earlier in the film after being caught gambling by his boss — Fred uses the Jetsons’ tech to help Mr. Slate’s chances in a competition at the company picnic. Mr. Slate agrees to give Fred his job back if he can secure a victory for Mr. Slate — unfortunately, even with a technological advantage, Mr. Slate still ends up losing, and fires Fred again.

During the Jetsons’ absence, Henry Orbit and Rosie the Robot Maid assemble a device to bring them back. The device locks onto the Flintstones instead, though, and brings the prehistoric cavemen into the future, stranding the Jetsons in the past. George Jetson manages to get himself a job with Mr. Slate, becoming his partner, and quickly becomes famous. Newly rich, the Jetsons buy multiple local businesses in town. In the future, the Jetsons’ robot helps manage to fix the time machine and Rosie the Robo Maid is sent back in time to retrieve her family. The Jetsons are brought back to the future, and the film ends with the Flintstones being sent back to their past.

In the 1990s, two video games would take inspiration from The Jetsons Meet the Flintstones , not only proving the success of the crossover but also continued interest in the conceit of the film. In 1991, NES released The Flintstones: The Rescue of Dino & Hoppy. To rescue the kidnapped pets, Fred Flintstone must reassemble a time machine and travel to the future. As part of the future stage, Fred Flintstone does end up running into George Jetson. In Flintstones/Jetsons Time Warp , a time machine sends Fred Flintstone to the future and George Jetson to the past.

In 1995, The Flintstones Meet the Jetsons was featured in an episode of The Simpsons . In “A Star is Burns”, an episode from The Simpsons’ sixth season, Bart Simpson is seen watching The Flintstones Meet the Jetsons.
