Thousands sought to make their voice heard, raising every issue from the difficulty of acquiring these unique cards, to protecting the sanctity of Magic: The Gathering ’s story IP. Many of these issues were solved, others dissolved—the IP problem for example can be compellingly reframed as a way to bring new players into the community. However, with the release of collaboration between Magic: The Gathering and iconic nerd property Star Trek , a brave new problem has reared its ugly head.

Sir Patrick Stewart was one of the most well-respected and well-liked actors of our era. His incredible acting range allowed him to feel at home playing parts in both critically acclaimed productions of Shakespeare, and in Elder Scrolls: Oblivion. His recent death sent shockwaves through a number of communities, and the thoughts of fans are still with the friends and family he left behind .&nbsp;

The design team at Wizard of the Coast could not have predicted this eventuality, and given his widespread fame it was only natural that his Star Trek character, Jean-Luc Picard, would appear on a number of cards, with 23 different cards representing Stewart’s likeness in their art. The cards include alternative art reprints of staples like Kenrith, the Returned King , the star of the “Federation Fleet" preconstructed deck , and one of the much sought after headliners—a copy of Captain Jean-Luc Picard personally signed by the actor himself.

The question raised is morbid but obvious; has Stewart’s tragic death unfairly and unnaturally buoyed the price of these already valuable cards? Online prices records seem to indicate the answer is yes. On October 28th, immediately following the passing of Sir Patrick Stewart, cards like Captain Jean-Luc Picard shot up from around 17 dollars to nearly 60 before dipping back down into the realm of reason.&nbsp;

The issue of signed cards is particularly thorny, as only 250 will ever be produced, and this will be one of the last items ever signed by the actor. As of time of writing, listings for the card have risen as high as $30,000. Any player of any trading card game can tell you that scalpers are a problem endemic to the genre—for them, this could be a goldmine, the perfect excuse to hike up the resale value. Scalpers must bulk buy their products from Wizards of Coast, meaning that both groups may in effect be profiting off of the death of Sir Patrick Stewart.

This highlights some of the biggest problems identified with Universes Beyond—When you use materials from franchises from a completely different medium, utilizing real world actors, you have much less control over your own press. As of yet, the actual effects of these unforeseen circumstances are completely unknown. If Wizards of the Coast takes extra measures in future to prevent this kind of disrespectful profiteering, the issue may be avoided entirely. It is certainly not the fault of any member of the team, and harassment of those involved would be an even bigger insult to the memory of a hero; as Picard said himself,&nbsp;“It is possible to commit no mistakes and still lose. That is not a weakness; that is life.”

The movie was based on the novel of the same name by Richard Matheson , and it had been made as a movie twice before with the titles The Last Man on Earth and The Omega Man . In a new interview on the Team Deakins podcast, director Francis Lawrence said, "I’m really proud of the first hour of that movie. The End of I Am Legend Betrays Its Core Themes Image Courtesy of Warner Bros. The name of the novel, I Am Legend , signifies that the hero of the story, Neville, realized that he was actually the monster in this world. He was hunting down the vampires and killing them during the day. However, readers were led to believe all the infected were like the undead vampires attacking his house, when there were also infected who were not undead. Neville believed he was the hero in his story, but he was only a murderer killing the new dominant species on the planet, a species that just wanted to survive in peace. However, the readers were led to believe they were evil because they kept tormenting Neville at his house. When he realized at the end that he was the monster and they were only fighting back. Lawrence's movie was all about Neville and other survivors finding a cure and fixing the vampire-zombie hybrids in the movie. By "fixing" something that wasn't a problem but was just the evolution of the species, it went completely against what this story was really about. Lawrence also talked about the comparison to the novel, and he said that an alternate ending played into it better. He also touched on how he had to change the ending, which originally had Neville leaving the infected woman to the monsters, while leaving with Anna and Ethan. He said test audiences hated that ending, and they had to change it one month before the film's release, with only three days of reshoots.

In the theatrical ending, Neville gives Anna and Ethan the cure, which works, and then he sacrifices himself with a grenade to kill the vampire-zombie hybrids who were attacking them. However, there was an alternate ending on the DVD that got it right, where Neville realizes that the infected are only trying to protect their own, and he returns the female companion he had captured for his experiments, and they allow him to live as a result. It still lost the idea that he was the monster, but it was at least closer.

In 2006, Nolan released the sci-fi thriller The Prestige , and it remains one of his best films and the highest-quality sci-fi movie of his career. The two men then set out on a mission to one-up each other, while Angier has more ominous plans in store for Borden, with help from real-life inventor Nikola Tesla (David Bowie), and a strong mix of sci-fi storytelling.

As with all Nolan's movies, this one plays with time.
