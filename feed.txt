They're trying to build a future together, even when their circumstances make that prospect seem increasingly ridiculous. That gives their decisions a stronger weight. A dangerous job isn't just another opportunity to make money or create some spectacular mess. It's something that could affect the person standing beside them, that's a natural concept for any human to feel, whether its a spouse, a family member, or anything between. I find that considerably more compelling than simply having another protagonist to switch between.

Jason and Lucia don't need to constantly agree with each other to make their partnership interesting, nor does their connection depend on turning every interaction into some grand romantic declaration. Rockstar has always been capable of writing memorable personalities, but giving those personalities a relationship worth investing in is a different challenge altogether. GTA 6 understands that distinction, and it makes Jason and Lucia considerably more interesting as a pair than either protagonist would be in isolation.

One of the things I find most interesting about Jason and Lucia is that their story doesn't have to stop whenever a mission ends. Its predecessor isn't some aging multiplayer mode that players have been desperately waiting to abandon. GTA Online has grown into an entertainment platform of its own, with an enormous amount of content and a community that has spent years making Los Santos their second home. Fortunately, there's a fairly straightforward way to approach this problem that SEGA has already experimented, with a positive result.

The challenge facing Rockstar isn't really about making a better version of GTA Online (though that's a given). It's the player base that leaving the original behind is worth it. I'd happily start from the bottom again if it meant having a new world to explore and a fresh set of criminal opportunities to pursue. This might sound like an unusual comparison, but SEGA's Phantasy Star Online 2: New Genesis offers Rockstar a surprisingly good blueprint.
