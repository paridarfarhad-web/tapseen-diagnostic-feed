&nbsp; Share on X (Opens in new window) X Share on Facebook (Opens in new window) Facebook Add Us On Google The Joker is the most iconic and terrifying supervillain in comic book history. For decades, the Clown Prince of Crime has terrorized Gotham and DC Comics . But despite his infamy, no one’s exactly sure who he is, including it seems the Joker himself. The Joker likes to say that he prefers his origin story to be a multiple choice, which has led to many possible explanations for his existence. No backstory has been proven true, and the Joker keeps making up new ones to keep things interesting. While we may never know the truth, these possible origins have shaped our understandings of the Joker.

With a character like the Joker, his origins can range from the tragic to the terrifying. Almost everyone tries, in some form, to explain how a monster like the Joker could have been created and how he started his eternal rivalry with Batman .

When Batman sat on the Mobius Chair and gained omnipotence during the “Darkseid War,” his first question was the Joker’s real identity. However, the Mobius Chair answered, “There are three.” And with that, DC Comics introduced the highly contentious retcon that there’s not one, but three Jokers. The controversial Three Jokers story explores this revelation, where Gotham is plagued by a trio of Jokers, each representing a different era of the character. These Jokers are the calculating Criminal of the Golden Age, the wacky Clown of the Silver Age who killed Jason Todd, and the psychopathic Comedian of the Bronze and Modern Ages who paralyzed Barbara Gordon. Naturally, it’s also suggested that two of these Jokers were created by the original, whichever one that may be. To this day, people argue over the canonicity of this story and which one of them, if any, is the true Joker. But then again, it’s very in-character for the Joker to make his own origin even more confusing.

Most possible Joker origins involve him getting soaked in toxic chemicals as an adult, which caused him to go insane. Yet Batman Vol. 2 #23.1 in 2013 gave the Joker a childhood so traumatic that it would have been impossible for him not to lose his mind. In this retelling, the Joker explains that, when he was a young boy, he lived with his abusive and murderous Aunt Eunice. The young boy was beaten, starved, and isolated every single day. But the worst revelation was that Aunt Eunice would regularly brush her nephew down with bleach. Naturally, this treatment would eventually lead to the Joker’s skin turning pure white. Aunt Eunice would also constantly gloat about how she brutally tortured and murdered her late husband to make her nephew even more terrified of her. Unlike other Joker origins that involve one terrible accident, his backstory in this issue shows how he was molded by the twisted woman who raised, abused, and disfigured him for years.

For all the death and destruction that the Joker causes, most people recognize that, at the end of the day, he’s still human. That was until the events of “Endgame,” which suggested the Clown Prince of Crime was something far older and more malevolent. The storyline proposes that for centuries, Gotham City has been plagued by a monster called the Pale Man who has claimed countless lives. The leading theory behind the Pale Man’s existence is that he was a man who came into contact with dionesium, an ingredient from Ra’s al Ghul’s Lazarus Pit. This substance made the person immortal, and he spent the next several centuries terrorizing the people of Gotham like a Boogeyman. What “Endgame” suggested was that the Joker is the Pale Man, and therefore an inhuman monster that can’t be killed. The Joker advocated this origin more than anyone. Although Batman eventually disproved this theory, it most likely played a part in inspiring the Absolute Joker.

One of the Joker’s most defining characteristics is his obsession with Batman. However, “Lovers and Madmen” turns this obsession into the catalyst that created the Clown Prince of Crime. In this retelling, the Joker is a ruthless bank robber named Jack who’s committed dozens of robberies. But after succeeding so many times, Jack grows bored with the profession. That was until a young Batman arrives and foils Jack’s latest scheme. Feeling he’d met a true rival and found a new purpose, Jack began committing bigger and deadlier robberies to get Batman’s attention. When Batman and Jack battle again, the Dark Knight throws a batarang that accidentally slices Jack’s mouth, giving him his iconic, gruesome grin. Batman gets so desperate to stop Jack’s rampage that he enlists the help of mobsters to track Jack down. When the mobsters try to execute Jack at a chemical factory, he escapes by falling into a vat that bleaches his skin white and turns his hair green. Since then, the Joker’s obsession with Batman hasn’t wavered.

When the Joker was first introduced, he was a complete enigma with no real name or backstory. DC Comics gave its very first possible origin for the Joker in Detective Comics #168 . There, the Joker explains that he was once a normal lab worker who decided to steal a million dollars from his employer, the Monarch Playing Card Company. To pull off the heist, the Joker created the Red Hood persona and costume. After stealing the money, the Joker swam through a sewer of chemical waste to avoid detection. The Red Hood helmet provided the Joker with an oxygen supply, so he didn’t drown. Unfortunately, when he emerged from the chemical waste and took off the helmet, the Joker discovered the chemicals had turned his body chalk white and his hair green. This revelation drove him mad and led him to become the Clown Prince of Crime. Most Joker origin stories use his former identity as the Red Hood and his dip in chemicals, so this revelation was highly influential.

When you dress up like a clown and kill innocent people for fun, chances are that you’re insane. Indeed, no matter how atrocious his actions are, the worst that ever happens to the Joker is that he’s thrown into Arkham Asylum because he’s considered to be legally insane. However, “Case Study” suggests it’s all a lie and that the Joker is a rational, natural-born monster. In notes written by Dr. Harleen Quinzel before her turn to villainy, she hypothesizes that the Joker was once a clever, elusive crime boss who killed for pleasure and to expand his territory. After carving his way through the criminal underworld, he created the Red Hood identity for the thrill. This led to his heist at a chemical plant, where, after Batman confronted him, he accidentally fell into a vat of acid. But where other versions of the Joker went mad from the deformity, this one only pretended to go insane. Now free from the threat of capital punishment, the Joker knows that nothing can stop his sadism.

No Joker story is more iconic than Alan Moore’s masterful yet controversial graphic novel The Killing Joke . This story not only reworked earlier ideas about the Joker’s origins but also added a new level of tragedy that led the Clown Prince of Crime to develop his terrifying philosophy. In this version, the Joker was once a kind but struggling comedian who desperately tried to make ends meet to support his pregnant wife. He was eventually approached by the mob, who offered him a lot of money to pretend to be the Red Hood while they robbed a chemical plant. But on the day of the robbery, the comedian learned that his wife died in a fire. This tragedy didn’t stop the mobsters from forcing the comedian to go through with the heist anyway. During the robbery, they were confronted by Batman, who assumed that the comedian was the ringleader. Their confrontation ended with he comedian falling into the vat of acid. This tragedy not only created the Joker but also led to his philosophy that all it takes is one bad day for someone to turn out just like him.

During EW's coverage of Avengers: Doomsday , Joe Russo didn't hold back about the scale of what he and brother Anthony Russo have built with Doomsday . "By far and away the most ambitious project we’ve ever attempted is the convergence of 25 years of storytelling, all the way back to the first X-Men in 2000," Russo said. Reed Richards actor Pedro Pascal echoed that same sentiment, stating, "It’s the biggest ensemble I’ve ever been a part of. To put us all in a room together, you would expect it to feel atomic. And yet it was like [an] immediate family. It was really warm. No one was alone; that was for sure.”

The meta-layers of Avengers: Doomsday are already apparent, as the film will be a bittersweet send-off for any number of Marvel movie franchises older fans grew up with, or enjoyed as pioneering adaptations of Marvel characters, before the MCU took the reins. Now, Doomsday and its sequel , Avengers: Secret Wars , will clear the board and reset the franchise, offering viewers a complete collection of Marvel characters all in one place, for the very first time. But that consolidated franchise will come at the cost of ending the arcs of beloved characters of yesteryear, and (more painfully), saying goodbye to some iconic performances, or (tragically, in the case of Patrick Stewart) some iconic performers.

However, Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars was unlike anything Marvel or DC had ever attempted in the past. This was the first major site-wide crossover series, and it changed everything about how comic books were made from that point on.

Even before Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars #1 (1984) by Jim Shooter and Michael Zeck came out, Marvel was already shaking up the entire brand in the most innovative way possible.

I was a kid when Marvel Comics prepared to start its Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars comic book series, and I had no idea what was going on. For most of my reading life, I was following mostly single and small multi-issue stories, and even when comic books crossed over, it was a big deal. DC Comics had the Justice League and Justice Society team up annually, and that was a huge event because these things had never happened before. When Marvel Super Heroes Contest of Champions came out in 1982, it was incredible, especially for a kid who had never dreamed he would see so many heroes fighting in one series.

What Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars did was take that idea and blow up the entire Marvel Universe. That is because Marvel showed before the series was even underway that it was going to change everything in Marvel and reset several major titles. This all started on the last page of most major comics published in January 1984, where the heroes went to Central Park and ended up disappearing without a trace. These moments in comics featuring Spider-Man, the X-Men, the Hulk, the Avengers, Captain America, Iron Man, and the Fantastic Four left it a mystery as to what happened.

What makes this so amazing is that the results of Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars were shown before the miniseries even hit its second issue. That is because all the heroes were back the next month, and there were major changes that happened without any explanation. First up, Spider-Man returned with a new black costume in The Amazing Spider-Man #252 , and Spider-Man could put it on and take it off by just commanding it with his mind. It took seven months before Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars #8 came out and showed him getting the costume, and it took even longer to prove this was a symbiote.

The Fantastic Four was back, but The Thing was no longer with them. In fact, the solo The Thing series had him on a barren planet having adventures while the Fantastic Four introduced the world to their newest team member, She-Hulk. It wasn't until Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars ended that The Thing's departure was explained. Colossus and Kitty Pryde's relationship was destroyed when he told Kitty about his relationship with Zsaji on Battleworld, which was a good thing thanks to their age difference. The miniseries took a while to show what Colossus did and who Zsaji even was.

Magneto came back with a new attitude, and Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars showed why he started his change from a villain to an antihero and eventually an actual X-Men member. The Hulk returned with an injured leg, and his intelligence was lessened a great deal, returning him more in line with his Savage Hulk persona. It was a huge reset for Magneto and the Hulk, and once again, it took several months to show what caused the changes in both men. Nothing like this had ever happened before in comics, and it changed everything in Marvel and DC.

In Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars , a cosmic entity known as the Beyonder captured heroes and villains from Earth and forced them to fight each other. This was all based on the Beyonder's curiosity to understand humans. He offered the winning side anything they wished for, which was very enticing for the villains. The heroes mostly wanted to just get back home. What happened next was a story of betrayals, intense battles, and awe-inspiring moments. The X-Men split off from the other heroes, refusing to trust anyone, while villains like Doctor Doom attempted to capture all the Beyonder's power for himself.

Spider-Man had his costume destroyed and found a machine that created a new one that he thought he controlled using his brainwaves. The Hulk had one of the most powerful moments in his history when he held up an entire mountain to save all the heroes. Spider-Man fought the X-Men and held his own to prove his power. By the end, everyone was back home except for The Thing, and nothing was the same.

This led to several similar series. Marvel released a sequel to Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars , although it had the Beyonder coming to Earth and disrupting life on the planet. Even more importantly, one year later, DC Comics released Crisis on Infinite Earths , which had a similar format where all the Earth's heroes had to stand up and unite to fight a being called the Anti-Monitor. In just two years, Marvel and DC each had a series that threw everyone into one comic book storyline and then shook up the entire universe when it was done.

In today's comic book world, there are often multiple site-wide crossover events every year. In 2026 alone, there have been "Avengers: Armageddon," "Queen in Black," and the upcoming "DNX." Titles like "Civil War," "Annihilation," "Secret Empire," and "House of M" wouldn't have existed without Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars . The 2015 "Secret Wars" event only exists because of this earlier series . Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars not only blew the mind of a kid who loved comics, but also changed how the major companies published their series.

&nbsp; Share on X (Opens in new window) X Share on Facebook (Opens in new window) Facebook Add Us On Google A joke from the original Twilight movie has been making the rounds ever since Robert Pattinson got cast as James Bond, and now the director has revealed that it was inspired by the way Pattinson looked on that day. In the scene, the character of Eric (Justin Chon) is announcing that the theme of the high school prom will be Monte Carlo, and one of the key elements will be "Bond. James Bond." The camera POV then moves to Pattinson's Edward, arriving to school in sunglasses with Kristen Stewart's Bella. On The Kyle Buchanan Show (as transcribed by Variety ), Twilight director Catherine Hardwicke revealed that the line was inspired by the way Pattinson looked in shades on set.

"That’s the one scene where I was like, ‘Let’s put the sunglasses on,'” she said. “He got out of the car and he looked so suave that he inspired the line, ‘Bond, James Bond.’ It was cool. We did think he looked like James Bond. I guess we were seeing the future." Four years later, in 2012, Pattinson said he would like to go for the role of Bond , but only when he hit the right age, saying, "It would be ridiculous to reinvent it as some young posh kid." ( Artemis Fowl tried the posh young kid approach, and proved Pattinson right.)

As the brooding, lonely vampire Edward Cullen, who falls for mortal Bella Swan but can't get intimate with her unless he turns her into a vampire too, Pattinson proved he could make masses of female fans swoon over him. Despite the competition from Taylor Lautner's abs, most of the self-professed Twi-hards were ultimately Team Edward. While The Twilight Saga proved a hit franchise on the strength of its female fan base, though, it was derided by critics and fans of more male-skewing franchises for its dedication to romance over world-building, and seemingly illogical changes to vampire mythology that had them sparkle in sunlight, and gave each one a different, X-Men like super power in addition to the immortality and blood-sucking.

Stewart had already proven her chops as a child actor, and immediately moved into more independent and artsy projects. Pattinson, on the other hand, had an uphill climb to prove to male fans that he wasn't just Edward Cullen. Working with directors like David Cronenberg and Christopher Nolan, he showed range and toughness, so that by the time he was cast as Batman, fans were only mildly skeptical, as opposed to when Michael Keaton and Ben Affleck got full-on hate. Once the movie was well-received, there was no question — if you can play Bruce Wayne, you can play James Bond. These days, it's Twilight that's being rebooted with a younger cast, and they can take on the roles without fear that they'll be typecast forever, knowing how their predecessors transcended it.
