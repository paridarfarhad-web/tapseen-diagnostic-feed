Fremantle Group COO and CEO Continental Europe Andrea Scrosati is stepping down after an eight-year tenure in which he oversaw an ambitious expansion of the Fremantle's international production operations, including the acquisition of multiple independent film and TV production companies.

Fremantle announced Friday that Scrosati, who joined the company from Sky Italia in 2018, and was named CEO Continental Europe in 2021, is stepping down to pursue a new entrepreneurial venture. He will stay on until the end of January 2027 to support the transition to new leadership.

During his tenure, Scrosati led Fremantle&#x2019;s mergers and acquisitions strategy, transforming the company from a business primarily associated with international entertainment formats into a global production group with a substantial presence in premium drama, independent film and documentaries.

Among the most significant acquisitions included Dublin-based Element Pictures, the production company behind The Favourite , Room and Normal People ; Italian producer Lux Vide, whose credits include Medici , Leonardo and Devils ; and the Asacha Media Group, which owns eight production labels across France, Italy, including Red Planet Pictures, the British producer of Death in Paradise , and Italy&#x2019;s Picomedia.

The acquisitions also provided the foundation for Fremantle&#x2019;s expansion in film. Among the major productions backed during Scrosati&#x2019;s tenure were Yorgos Lanthimos&#x2019; Poor Things ; Joanna Hogg&#x2019;s The Eternal Daughter ; Ali Abbasi&#x2019;s The Apprentice ; and Sebasti&#xE1;n Lelio&#x2019;s The Wonder . During Scrosati's tenure, Fremantle racked up 17 Oscar nominations and four Academy Awards for its titles.

Scrosati also oversaw Fremantle&#x2019;s expansion into documentaries and sports programing, the growth of its free ad-supported streaming television (FAST) channel business and the creation of Imaginae Studios, a standalone production label focused on artificial intelligence. Its first production, Art Awakens , launched in 2026.

The M&A activity was part of a broader growth strategy set by Fremantle's parent company RTL Group, which in 2021 set an ambitious target of doubling Fremantle&#x2019;s annual revenue to &#x20AC;3 billion ($3.3 billion) within four years, through acquisitions and the expansion of Fremantle&#x2019;s production businesses. The company did not reach that goal. Revenue peaked at approximately &#x20AC;2.27 billion in 2023 before falling to &#x20AC;2.254 billion in 2024 and &#x20AC;2.043 billion in 2025, leaving it nearly &#x20AC;1 billion short of the original target. RTL has subsequently pushed the &#x20AC;3 billion ambition further into the future.

"Andrea has made an enormous contribution to Fremantle over the past eight years," Jennifer Mullin, Global CEO Fremantle, said in a statement. "He has been instrumental in our growth and transformation, from helping build Fremantle into a true portfolio company and leading our M&A strategy, to establishing our film strategy and constantly pushing us to think differently about innovation and the future of our business."

Scrosati said the time was right to move on and focus on building something new. &#x201C;I leave with enormous pride in what we have achieved together and great affection for the company and everyone who makes it what it is."

Players are still finding secrets in Red Dead Redemption 2 , so it'll likely be years before every corner of GTA 6 has been similarly pried for information. One Easter Egg most players may have already found is a truly surprising scene of a dog driving a jet ski out into the oceans of GTA 6 as you're swimming. You might be scuba diving, driving a jet ski yourself, or simply exploring beyond beach shores before this dog pulls up next to you in a random encounter. Whether you're Jason or Lucia, you can question this dog, but they aren't exactly going to provide any answers right away. Instead, you can hop into the jet ski with the dog, and travel across the waves in style as you see some beautiful scenery through their capable paws.

Sticking with the dog will have you travel all along the coastal parts of Leonida, but the Leondia Keys is mainly where you'll find this Easter Egg the most. It seems like a bunch of dogs have created their own secret hideout of sorts, with multiple dogs there with life jackets, sunglasses, and otherwise cool clothing that lets you know what kind of club they run.

The jet ski dog will eventually take you to their island if you stick with them, where you can play with the dogs there until you want to leave. This isn't a very layered Easter Egg, but it does break up the usual sights of Vice City in GTA 6 with something you aren't likely to expect. Accessing Photo mode when this happens is almost a must, as this rare ride along with arguably the smoothest character in GTA 6 is something that only takes place once.

Players can find an actual cryptid in GTA 6 , mirroring the appearance of other strange creatures that have shown up in past games throughout the franchises. Games like GTA: San Andreas created an Easter Egg with the Loch Ness monster, while other titles had other wild figures like the "Ratman," or even Bigfoot themselves. A tall, wild Bigfoot is the most famous American cryptid, but it shows up in GTA 6 as something you can see and chase down yourself. However, it is named the "Skunk Ape" in GTA 6 , for the trail of mysterious gas it seems to emit as it moves around.

This large, hairy, bipedal cousin of Bigfoot creature can be located around in Grassrivers area of the map, again showing up as a random encounter. There are several shrines and effigies scattered around this area showing some sort of worship toward the Skunk Ape, typically made out of straw and rope in the forests there. By hanging around a Skunk Ape shrine, the creature might pop up unexpectedly, being something you can chase into the woods until the gas it creates eventually knocks your character out. You should be able to see the Skunk Ape hunting and feeding on local wildlife like deer, making it easier to hear and spot.

Even if you don't go unconscious from chasing the Skunk Ape, the creature has some sort of weird power that allows it to turn into a tree or simply vanish from sight. At some point in your pursuit, the cryptid will vanish, leaving behind nothing. That being said, there are quests in the game tied to spotting this creature, starting with early sightings with Jason as he explores the areas where the Skunk Ape could appear. By talking to the NPC Kirk, a cryptid hunter, you should be able to turn this chase into something that grants a few rewards.

The fictional series Dexter is about a serial killer in a bay area, in a location very similar to the Florida-inspired region of Leonida that GTA 6 takes place in. The Bay Harbor Butcher may be alive and well in GTA 6 through a recently found Easter Egg within Vice City, the largest area in the game. There is an apartment complex in the Washington Island sub-area of Vice City, close to the marked spots for Vice Beach Top Fun Drones and Pay N' Spray, two vendor locations. From here, going northeast from Penmarch Isle should take you to a series of buildings named Trinity Apartments, which may alert Dexter fans.

The Trinity Killer was one of the most notorious villains from Season 4 of Dexter , so players should be drawn to the GTA 6 apartments possibly referencing the other serial killer. Going to one of the upper corner apartment buildings here will show a room with all of its shades shuttered, in an eerie contrast to the other residents near that space. By going to the roof of the building, you can find all of Dexter Morgan's gear, including a series of syringes he uses to knock out his soon-to-be victims. Here, you can also find a knife, some rope, gloves, multiple tool boxes, a rag, and some handcuffs indicating the killer's "process."

In the distance from the roof, you may also spot a boot that looks similar to Dexter's "Slice of Life" vessel, making the Easter Egg even more complete than you would think. This second floor space is not one to interact with as much as the jet ski dog or Skunk Ape, but it does provide a nice homage to another series with a similar aesthetic to the game. With fan theories already pointing toward the character Mandy as being a possible serial killer herself, the Dexter references make even more sense as you play.

GTA 6 , on the other hand, has Rockstar finally give the alien rumors more attention than just a UFO on a poster card or a fleeting encounter with the extraterrestrial as seen in games like GTA 5 . In this game, players can actually get abducted by aliens, even getting a chance to travel inside a UFO should they control the right character during that situation. GTA 6 is a game that will likely keep adding Easter Eggs as new updates come and go, making it a content-rich title players will be exploring for years.

&nbsp; Share on X (Opens in new window) X Share on Facebook (Opens in new window) Facebook Add Us On Google FromSoftware is one of gaming's most recognizable names, largely because of the addictive difficulty of its games. Dark Souls and Elden Ring have given it widespread acclaim, while Bloodborne remains one of the most beloved titles of all time. Even now, fans eagerly await its Nintendo Switch 2 exclusive, The Duskbloods . However, some of FromSoftware's most interesting ideas haven't been explored in decades. And one recent indie developer shows just how influential those games were and how they can capture the same success as FromSoftware's soulslike entries, especially considering they were responsible for the genre's creation.

King's Field is one of FromSoftware's most underrated games and largely helped the soulslike genre form. Yet, despite how important it is, the series has all but disappeared. That is until indie developer Freshly Baked Games released Queen's Domain . The reception from reviewers and players has been incredible, showing that there is still an audience for this type of dungeon crawler. It is also a strong argument that FromSoftware should return to King's Field and revive one of its legacy franchises with modern hardware and standards.

Long before FromSoftware became known for its punishing boss fights, it proved it could create immersive and engaging fantasy worlds with King's Field . Released in 1994 for the original PlayStation, King's Field was FromSoftware's first video game. It established many of the characteristics the studio is known for, such as oppressive environments, cryptic storytelling, unforgiving enemies, and minimal guidance. Even the Moonlight Sword, which has appeared throughout FromSoftware's history in various forms, was first introduced here.

The influence of King's Field on the soulslike genre is something Hidetaka Miyazaki himself has acknowledged. While Demon's Souls wasn't developed as a sequel, it certainly feels like an expanded version of the formula. There are clear similarities between the games, particularly in how they encourage players to overcome challenges through patience, experimentation, and knowledge of their surroundings rather than overwhelming enemies with brute force.

One of my favorite things about FromSoftware's RPGs is their storytelling, particularly how the environment contributes to this. There is something special about standing outside a dark tunnel, debating whether whatever treasure lies inside is worth risking your progress and life. King's Field capitalized on that feeling through its first-person perspective, creating a degree of vulnerability that distinguishes it from modern soulslike games. Unfortunately, the series hasn't received a mainline entry since King's Field IV in 2001.

But on October 30th, 2026, King's Field fans got the next best thing in Queen's Domain . Players explore the mysterious Isle of Neasied while searching for their missing father, discovering secrets, collecting weapons, and battling monsters. Its retro graphics, stamina-based combat, and labyrinthine environments make it feel immediately like King's Field , but this isn't just nostalgia bait. The game is genuinely incredible and deserves all its praise.

But the game isn't just copying FromSoftware. Queen's Domain gives players the Holy Sword Endu, which does more than just slay enemies. It gives players aerial movement abilities that transform how they explore environments, almost leaning into a Metroidvania feel. Revisiting locations after gaining upgrades unveils new secrets and keeps players on their toes. Progression across Queen's Domain is incredibly rewarding and goes beyond just being influenced by FromSoftware and creating its own identity.

The reception speaks for itself. Freshly Baked Games announced that Queen's Domain sold 100,000 copies within five days of release. It also boasts an Overwhelmingly Positive rating on Steam across 5,600 reviews. The appeal of this style of game cannot be understated enough. Other games like this have come and gone, such as Dread Delusion , but Queen's Domain demanded attention, and it has absolutely received it.

FromSoftware returning to an older franchise wouldn't be a surprise. Armored Core VI: Fires of Rubicon revived the series in 2023 after nearly a decade of being dormant. Rather than forcing its soulslike formula onto the franchise, FromSoftware kept the game to its series' roots while implementing modern elements from its current franchises. This is absolutely the direction a new King's Field game has to go, and it is past time that we receive a new game in this cult classic series.

Exploring an interconnected kingdom with the environmental detail of Elden Ring , the unsettling atmosphere of Bloodborne , and the first-person exploration of King's Field . Valor Mortis proved a first-person soulslike could work , but FromSoftware can take this to another level. The studio's responsive combat, sophisticated enemy behavior, expanded magic systems, and environmental puzzles with a King's Field name would be a dream come true.

Sadly, FromSoftware and Miyazaki have expressed reservations about reviving King's Field , though this was back in 2016. But Queen's Domain is a great example of why revisiting the past and taking risks is important. FromSoftware should not abandon soulslike games, but it has the bandwidth and resources to diversify its library. A new King's Field is the perfect way to do that, and it would even minimize the risk considering the series has an established foundation.

Bitch Planet ran for ten issues between 2014 and 2017, with a follow-up anthology series called Bitch Planet: Triple Feature , which ran for five issues. Each Triple Feature contained three stories from different writers and artists, including many of the top creatives in the industry today. Bitch Planet technically never ended. Both DeConnick and de Landro have expressed wanting to go back to it, but never quite found the right time . The themes of incarceration and feminism only got more and more relevant over the years, and DeConnick has mentioned at conventions that the book is hitting a little too close to home. Bitch Planet takes place in a dystopian reality where not only are non-compliant women sent to prison, but they are also sent to prison in space. Crudely referred to as Bitch Planet both in-universe and in the comics’ title, the Auxiliary Compliance Outpost is exactly what readers would expect.&nbsp;

DeConnick created Bitch Planet out of a love for the 1960s and 1970s’ women's prison movies and wanted to spotlight the intriguing and difficult aspects of women’s lives in prison. Nudity is common throughout the comic, but it isn’t meant to be cheesecakey or serve as eye candy. Instead, it’s meant to help flesh out these characters. One of the most striking moments from the first issue of Bitch Planet is the leader of Auxiliary Compliance Outpost explaining that the prison jumpsuits are one-size-fits-all when that clearly isn’t the case. One woman struggles to put on, given that it barely has room for just one of her tits.&nbsp;

Prison media in general is packed with existential dread — prisoners are forcibly removed from society, stripped of their rights and belongings, and often feel trapped. However, Bitch Planet takes it to a whole new level by transporting them to a different planet. A husband of one of the prisoners, Maria Collins, explains that people never hear of escapees from Bitch Planet , emphasizing the prison as a dead end. Besides, even if they did escape, where would they go?&nbsp;

Shows like Orange Is the New Black — which the creators avoided watching in fear of taking too much inspiration from — inevitably allow for reintegration into society by the end. The women are seen as beyond correction, and it is for the good of Father Earth — an interesting subversion given how Earth is typically referred to as a Mother — that they are sent away, lest a sickness spread.&nbsp;

As written in the afterword of the first issue of Bitch Planet , “the most striking thing about Bitch Planet is that we’re already on it.” Bitch Planet was unafraid of social commentary, both in and out of its story, remarking on not only sexism, but racism, fatphobia, and the intersections that they cross.
