&nbsp; Share on X (Opens in new window) X Share on Facebook (Opens in new window) Facebook Add Us On Google When thinking of Batman vehicles it is hard to get past the Batmobile as the most iconic of the Dark Knight's rides. That doesn't mean that the character doesn't have other ways of getting around Gotham City that are just as awesome. In 1989's Tim Burton movie, the Batwing created one of the franchise's unforgettable images when Michael Keaton's Batman flies it in front of a full moon, creating a version of the Bat Signal in the sky. Keaton also got to use the Batskiboat in Batman Returns while the Batboat was piloted by Robin in Batman Forever . Yet it was Christian Bale who had the coolest Batmobile alternative with the Batpod, a two-wheeled motorcycle that is slim and sleek and creates quite an impression blasting around the narrow alleys of the city. Fans of the Christopher Nolan-directed Batman trilogy now have the option of getting their hands on the actual vehicle used in The Dark Knight , but you may not be able to afford it on an honest wage.

Used in The Dark Knight and The Dark Knight Rises , the Batpod is used by Bale's Batman in several key scenes across the Nolan's sequels. In The Dark Knight , the Batpod is used after the Tumbler vehicles is wrecked with Batman almost driving it into Heath Ledger's Joker but diverting around him at the last moment. It is also seen in the failed attempt to rescue Maggie Gyllenhaal's Rachel and in the search for Aaron Eckhart's Harvey Dent. The vehicle was then used again in The Dark Knight Rises when Batman confronts Bane and the League of Shadows. Thanks to several distinct scratches and other markings, the Batpod has been matched to the Joker scene in The Dark Knight , which is one reason this is already bidding at $450,000 with an eye on pushing towards $1 million.

In Nolan's Dark Knight trilogy, the trusty long and iconic Batmobile is replaced with the Tumbler, an armored stocky vehicle that can blast around Gotham and withstand most attacks from the city's criminals. Designed by Nolan and production designer Nathan Crowley, the Batpod was conceived as a smaller alternate vehicle for Bale's crimefighter to use when the moment requires something slim and powerful. As well as appearing in the movie, the Batpod was also used in many promotional events for both movies, making this a very special piece of Batman history for anyone looking for their new ultimate collectible. Although it may not be the same as owning the Batmobile - the 1992 Batman Returns movie version recently sold for almost $5 million in a separate auction - owning any of the Dark Knights array of vehicles will see this one attracting a lot more bids before the hammer falls.

The custom-made Batpod is built around a Honda CRF450X engine, with 31&#8243; Hoosier racing tyres, fibreglass body panels, plastic, resin and fibreglass twin cannon barrels at the front of the bike, a light-up laser targeting unit and a breaking system operated by the user from the handlebar controls. However, don't expect to go rocketing around the streets on this bad boy as the vehicle comes in "rolling only" condition, with the battery, fuel tank and throttle controls all having been removed. There are certainly some collectibles that would make you the envy of your friends, and this must be one of the biggest. Even without the ability to actually drive it, this is still a very rare opportunity to own one of the coolest Batman vehicles, and just being able to sit on the same seat as Christian Bale across two movies should be enough for any serious collector to reach for their wallet.

&nbsp; Share on X (Opens in new window) X Share on Facebook (Opens in new window) Facebook Add Us On Google Spider-Man has one of the strongest rogues galleries in comics , only rivaled by other genre-defining classics like Batman and Daredevil. Green Goblin, Venom, Doctor Octopus, and so many more have become staples who have stood at the center of some of the greatest stories Marvel has ever put to page, but they're far from the only foes this Wall-Crawler deals with on a bi-weekly basis. He has dozens of villains who could have just as much potential to make a big splash, but they've been relegated to a scant few appearances, if they show up at all outside of their debuts. Today, we're giving those Spider-Man villains their due and digging into what makes them so interesting. Without further ado, let's swing into Spider-Man's supervillain situation.

Vermin's life has always been on a downward spiral. First, his father abused him terribly as a child, and even after getting away from that house, he wound up being captured by Baron Zemo and experimented on, turning him into a human-rat hybrid. All of this left the man with a serious case of self-worth issues, which means that he's always manipulated or used by other supervillains in their schemes. He's a tragic villain in a way that not many of Spider-Man's most popular villains are, which could be a great platform to explore the humanity and understanding that makes Spider-Man such a great hero.

From a powers standpoint, Vermin is also one of the few who can match Spidey in terms of pure agility. He might not be as strong as Pete, but he makes up for it with ferocity and rat-like senses, which even let him command other small animals, like rats and stray dogs, to attack like a small army. Speaking of armies, Vermin also has the ability to create duplicates of himself, though when he last used this, it cost him his intellect. Overall, Vermin has perfect rat theming , which contrasts Spider-Man's spider theme in a fantastic and very entertaining way.

Digger is only one name of this undead creature, who is otherwise known as the Vegas Thirteen. He's called that because he's actually a composite being made up of different body parts from thirteen of the deadliest, evilest crime bosses and their lieutenants, stitched together and resurrected by gamma radiation. Digger is a fascinating character because he captures that classic man-out-of-time energy that makes Captain America work so well, while combining it with classic gangster style and theatrics. There's so much more potential for a concept like this than his scant few appearances, none of which dug into this as much as it deserves.

Of course, Digger also offers a unique challenge for Spider-Man, given that he's really, really strong. While not quite on the Hulk's level, Digger is on a completely different level than most of Spider-Man's other foes, to the point where Peter couldn't hurt him if he tried. This forces Spider-Man to get creative in his approach, which is always when he's at his best. The greatest Spider-Man fights involve Peter coming up with an outside-the-box solution like only he can, and this is one problem that he absolutely cannot brute force his way through.

Shathra is the Totem-eating Wasp-Goddess that hails from the same realm as Morlun, although instead of being a vampire, she's much more willing to directly consume her prey. Her ultimate goal is to destroy the Web of Life and Destiny and replace it with one of her own design, often bringing her into conflict with the various defenders of the Spider-Verse. Unlike Morlun, however, she's much more insidious in her hunts and enjoys playing with her food. The first time she battled Spider-Man, she transformed into a human woman and attempted to ruin his reputation before finally destroying him, which just goes to show that she revels in both the hunt and the cruelty she inflicts.

Much like how Spider-Man can do everything a spider can do, Shathra can do everything a moth can. She's also shown to be physically outclass Spider-Man in just about everything, possess shapeshifting powers, and is even able to infect others to join her villainous army of wasps. Shathra is a different kind of Spider-Verse monster, mixing the predatory fear that Morlun generates with the cruel, calculating cunning more akin to someone like Norman Osborn, and those two coming together, along with the great imagery of a wasp and spider fight, make her a fantastic candidate for future adventures.

Tendril started out as a man down on his luck. After a lifetime of tragedy, between his mother killing herself, his dad losing himself to mental disorders, and bouncing around foster homes, he thought he would meet his end at the hands of flesh-eating bacteria. He volunteered for experimental testing of a cure, only for Peter's own lab work to get accidentally used on him, transforming him into a monstrous form where his entire body was made of super-strong webbing. He became a spider-life figure who was practically immune to any and all physical harm, possessing all of the strengths and adaptability of Spider-Man's greatest creation amplified tenfold.

Spider-Man fighting a villain literally made of his webbing is the type of idea that's either the dumbest thing of all time or the most interesting, and either way, it definitely deserves to get explored a bit more in the future. However, as entertaining as Tendril's powers are, his real contribution to the Spider-mythos is definitely his connection to Peter. The Wall-Crawler would definitely blame himself for everything that happened, and Peter is always at his most interesting when he's grappling with his conscience on some level . This could force Peter to confront some aspects of himself that he definitely does not like, and it could be really awesome to see.

Molten Man is one of the all-time classic Spider-Man villains, debuting all the way back in The Amazing Spider-Man (1963) #28. A failed robbery ended up with Mark Raxton being coated in an experimental liquid alloy that turned his skin to gold and superheated him to the point that everything he touched burst into flames. He learned that he could control this fire, too, by shooting off massive blasts and fireballs practically whenever he wanted. He's a walking threat, given that just being near him is enough to blister your skin, and his actual touch could melt a person in seconds. Currently, he's retired, as he's been cured of his condition, but he definitely doesn't deserve to be forgotten.

See, the most interesting part about Molten Man is that he's the step-brother of longtime Spidey character Liz Allen, which opens the doors for all kinds of familial drama that I'm surprised we haven't seen yet. For example, despite Liz literally being married to Harry, we've never had a Green Goblin and Molten Man team up. There's an untapped well of potential stories here, especially considering the only things in the world that Mark cares about are his sister and finding a cure. This could easily be pushed to any number of logical extremes that would be sure to give us some wonderfully resonant stories.

&nbsp; Share on X (Opens in new window) X Share on Facebook (Opens in new window) Facebook Add Us On Google Nick Frost plays Hagrid in the upcoming Harry Potter HBO series, and he has named two of his main inspirations for how he played the character. Neither of them is the original Hagrid actor, Robbie Coltrane. Frost is best known to fans as the actor who starred in Edgar Wright's Three Flavours Cornetto Trilogy with Simon Pegg. These movies, Shaun of the Dead , Hot Fuzz , and The World's End , made him a cult favorite, and this makes his move to the Harry Potter world such a huge step up for the actor. He is also trying to make the role his own.

In a special Q&A from the official Harry Potter website, Frost explained that there were two figures who inspired his portrayal of Hagrid in the Harry Potter HBO series. He said the two inspirations were John Coffey, the character that Michael Clarke Duncan played in the movie The Green Mile , and Frost's own Uncle Emy.

According to Frost, the first inspiration for his portrayal of Hagrid in the HBO Harry Potter series was the character John Coffey from The Green Mile . This was a movie based on a Stephen King novel about a man wrongly found guilty of murder and sentenced to die on death row. That man was John Coffey, and he was a gentle giant who had magical powers to heal others but who was willing to accept his fate despite his innocence. According to Frost, Coffey was "a very, very dangerous man, but he's essentially a big child who can be hurt."

The second inspiration is one that only Frost and his family knew about. It was his uncle, Emy. He said that Emy was a youngster in the 1950s who contracted an obscure strain of scarlet fever, and he never mentally or physically progressed past the age of 11. Frost continued by saying, "When me and my cousins spent every holiday down in Wales with him (my gran lived in this huge forest), he was like the Pied Piper. We’d just follow him around. He’d tell us stories he’d made up or take us to a den that he’d built or a little bit of river where he had dammed. Sometimes he’d have a chick in his pocket, keeping it warm. That was magic to me. I wanted Hagrid to be that."

In the Harry Potter novels, Hagrid was an innocent giant when he attended Hogwarts, and that allowed Tom Riddle (the future Voldemort) to frame him for opening the Chamber of Secrets and causing the death of a Muggle-born student, Moaning Myrtle. Hagrid's innocence and lack of intelligence didn't allow him to understand how to defend himself, and he was expelled. Luckily, Dumbledore believed in his innocence, and he convinced then-Headmaster Armando Dippet to let Hagrid remain at Hogwarts as a gamekeeper, although he was no longer allowed to legally practice magic.

Frost explained that he wanted his version of Hagrid to have the innocence that people like his Uncle Emy and John Coffey from The Green Mile exhibited. He added that Hagrid was big and strong enough to hurt someone, and he had an edge of danger to him, but he would only hurt someone if they tried to hurt anyone he cared about, like young Harry Potter.

Director Frank Oz had recently welcomed a child and wanted to spend some time with his family. Unfortunately, the technically ambitious production was already running behind schedule. Moranis stepped in for Oz, inadvertently making the director look bad in the process.

In a rare interview with Empire celebrating the movie's 40th anniversary, Moranis explained that he had the luxury of shooting material that didn't involve the movie's famously elaborate man-eating plant. Oz was regularly spending entire days on just a handful of camera setups because of the technical demands of bringing Audrey II to life. Without having to contend with the enormous puppet, Moranis was able to get through considerably more material. The actor managed to get through between 12 and 14 setups while filling in for the director. Apparently, Oz still gives Moranis a hard time about how productive he was during his brief stint directing the movie. Directing wasn't the only skill Moranis had to put to use on Little Shop of Horrors . While the actor was already an accomplished comedic performer, he was determined to do his own singing in the adaptation of Howard Ashman and Alan Menken's stage musical.

Few directors in Hollywood were better equipped to tackle the technical challenges of Little Shop of Horrors than Oz. The longtime collaborator of Jim Henson had already co-directed The Dark Crystal , directed The Muppets Take Manhattan , and brought Yoda to life in two Star Wars movies. Even with his considerable experience as a puppeteer, getting Audrey II to convincingly perform musical numbers proved to be a challenge.

The larger versions of Audrey II couldn't move fast enough to keep up with the vocals provided by the Four Tops' Levi Stubbs. Oz compensated by filming the puppet at a lower frame rate, allowing its movements to be sped up when the footage was projected at a standard frame rate. Unfortunately for Moranis, that meant the actor had to sing and move in slow motion whenever he shared the frame with Audrey II during those musical numbers. Even something as simple as walking required Moranis to rethink how he was using his body.

The production would face an entirely different challenge when test audiences rejected the movie's original ending . Oz had remained relatively faithful to the stage musical, which saw Seymour and Audrey, Ellen Greene, meet their demise before Audrey II and its offspring took over the world. Audiences had grown so attached to Moranis and Greene over the course of the movie that they weren't particularly receptive to watching the pair get eaten. Oz ultimately gave audiences the happy ending they wanted, though fans can now watch the movie with its original ending restored.

Oz would reunite with Moranis just four years later for My Blue Heaven , a comedy that afforded the director the luxury of working with Moranis and Steve Martin without having to accommodate an enormous puppet. As much as Oz might still give Moranis grief about his productivity behind the camera on Little Shop of Horrors , he clearly enjoyed having him in front of it. Audiences will finally get to see Moranis back on the big screen in 2027 when he reprises his role as Dark Helmet in Spaceballs 2 . &nbsp; Share on X (Opens in new window) X Share on Facebook (Opens in new window) Facebook Add Us On Google Despite The Simpsons still running to this day on television, its run in the video game medium has been relatively nonexistent as of late, outside of various and rare crossovers . This drought was seemingly coming to a close with a heavily rumored remake of the fan-favorite PS2, Xbox, PC, and GameCube title The Simpsons: Hit & Run . However, it seems as though this unannounced remake has crashed and burned.

As gathered by Video Games Chronicle , Darren Evenson, senior game designer at New Radical Games, posted vaguely about a canceled game on LinkedIn . He referenced a "fantastic project with an absolutely incredible team" that wasn't able to escape the "current state of the industry right now." He then pointed the finger at those at the top with the money and their unwillingness to fund promising games.

"Oh, you silly suits with spreadsheets… will you EVER learn?" said Evenson. "If you don't make the game, you can't make the money… and if you don't make the money, then you don't get the power… and… well, there's more to that quote, but I digress."

Nothing in that post explicitly references The Simpsons: Hit & Run . As Video Games Chronicle pointed out, there's possibly a sly reference to The Simpsons show in the LinkedIn post through the "will you EVER learn" part . It's maybe a bit of a stretch, but, even if that specific connection is tenuous, there was other evidence to support that Evenson was indeed speaking to The Simpsons: Hit & Run .

For one, New Radical Games worked on the original game back in 2003, albeit under a different name. Secondly, series creator Matt Groening said at a D23 panel back in August that he thought "the original game is coming back in some form" until showrunner Matt Selman interjected "or not," seemingly trying to cast doubt over something that hadn't been officially unveiled yet. Selman also helped write The Simpsons: Road Rage and Hit & Run .

Selman also contributed to the hubbub surrounding a Hit & Run remaster or remake. In March, he acknowledged the love for the game and didn't shoot down the idea of it being resurrected in some form.

"Nothing is set in stone," said Selman. "But my quote about Hit & Run would be, ‘Never say never,’ because we know people love it. We know they want it, so that’s good. If we know people want it, never say never.”

This Grand Theft Auto-esque game has stayed in players' hearts for a few reasons. For one, there hasn't been any traditional non-mobile Simpsons video games since 2007's The Simpsons Game . And it's been just a little over 20 years since Hit & Run 's release, which is just enough time for nostalgia to burn at its brightest.

Even though fans have tried to come up with their own solutions through mods and unofficial remakes , official plans to make another Hit & Run have fallen through. According to producer John Melchior, the publisher had reportedly rejected the deal to do more games despite Hit & Run 's success, echoing something other developers have said in the past. With stalled sequels and an allegedly canned remake, it seems as though it will be quite tough to see any non-mobile video games set in the world of the iconic animated series coming in the near future.

Since the new glitch essentially breaks the mid-game without much effort, is also likely to instill a greater sense of urgency in Rockstar for a patch. The prior infinite money glitch required a significant amount of monotonous repetition and travel time to farm it. This one, on the other hand, does not - which means players who want to give it a go should test it out ASAP.

The new glitch is even "buggier" than the previous one - and requires players to progress through a series of animations that duplicate the value of the stash bags that Lucia and Jason use during robberies. Repeating the animations essentially duplicates the value of the bags again - until they max out at $130,000.

The new glitch essentially stacks on top of the other infinite money glitches , which required players to repeatedly revisit locations and collect valuables that infinitely respawned. It's not required that players visit one of these locations to get an initial burst of funds from the glitch but to keep performing it, gamers will need an easily accessible source of valuables to trigger the bags to spawn over and over.

Once both bags are maxed out, you can sell them as usual or continue farming by stashing them in your vehicle and picking up another batch of valuables (to trigger another set of bags). If you intend to do the duplication glitch multiple times, you should pick up less money, valuables, and drugs at each location - you'll spend a bit more time duplicating but less time traveling to reset the farm.
