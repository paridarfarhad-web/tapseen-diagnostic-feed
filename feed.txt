This was true with everyone from Batman to Daredevil, and in every case, the stories found a way to tie the themes of family and fellowship into the story of a superhero trying to bring down a supervillain to save the holidays. Of course, Thanksgiving is an American holiday in November, and the stories that come out concerning that special day are almost always lined up to coincide with the celebration.

What makes many of the Thanksgiving superhero stories so great is that the heroes are either trying to celebrate with their families or helping make sure that others can celebrate in peace. The bad thing is, villains don't care, and if they disrupt family dinners, all the better.

The team of child heroes Power Pack had a pretty emotional Thanksgiving storyline that played out in Power Pack #18-19 (1985) by Louise Simonson, Brent Anderson, and Scott Williams. The two stories were titled "Kurse" and "Guess Who's Coming to Dinner," and while the first issue was about the superhero battles on Thanksgiving, the second was about the fallout for the Power family. This was a tough time because their mother was in the hospital, and most of the Powers kids didn't want to celebrate Thanksgiving at all.

Issue #18 was a "Secret Wars II" crossover where the Beyonder-powered Dark Elf Kurse injured their mom, and the kids were unable to beat him until Thor and Beta Ray Bill showed up to help. Young Katie invited several of their superhero friends over for Thanksgiving dinner, but this angered Alex, who called her selfish for wanting this while their mother was hurt. However, when Katie explained that she wanted the heroes who are always alone to be together on the holiday, it caused everyone to understand what family really meant.

Action Comics #673 (1991) by Roger Stern and Bob McLeod had Clark Kent and Lois Lane celebrating Thanksgiving with the Lane family, only for them to find out that Jimmy Olsen is now homeless after getting fired from the Daily Planet, and he is on the streets away from his friends on this holiday. Superman heads out to find Jimmy, only to end up in a giant battle with "Ugly" Mannheim, the former Intergang leader, who breaks out of jail, arms himself with a rock-like suit and a nuclear device, and plans to destroy Metropolis.

Superman has to fight and defeat Mannheim before his plans can take form, and he never does find where Jimmy Olsen is. The issue does show that Jimmy finds some other people to celebrate with, as he joins Bibbo Bibbowski for a Thanksgiving party at the Ace O' Clubs, a bar that Bibbo had bought to help people living in the Suicide Slum. It was a touching story, and while Superman couldn't find Jimmy, at least the young man had someone to help take him in.

Spider-Gwen #14 (2016) by Jason Latour and Robbi Rodriguez was a Thanksgiving story that touched on both the holiday and the idea of family during this time of the year. The story was titled "Cold Turkey," and it had Gwen in a tough spot, as her father, George Stacy, was in jail, and she ended up having to make a deal with Earth-65's crime lord, Matt Murdock, who agreed to get her father freed for her. The cover teased what Matt was going to bring to Thanksgiving dinner.

Spider-Gwen almost got her Thanksgiving dinner with Jessica Drew and her family, but things got cut short when the Koala Kommander attacked the Thanksgiving Day parade, and she had to save the day.

Daredevil #178 (1981) by Frank Miller and Klaus Janson was a Thanksgiving superhero battle that involved several heroes, first fighting each other and then battling the real villains, all during the Thanksgiving Day parade. Villains in the issue also included Kingpin, Turk, and Grotto, while Elektra meets with the Kingpin about a job in his organization, setting up her turn as his assassin, a huge turning point in the series.

One of the most iconic Thanksgiving superhero stories of all time came in the Batman comic book series Batman: The Long Halloween . This was issue #2 by Jeph Loeb and Tim Sale, and it followed the Holiday killer, whose gimmick was killing people on specific holidays. In this case, Mickey "The Mink" Sullivan's Irish crew confesses to bombing Harvey Dent and his wife, Gilda, but refuses to give up The Roman. They post bail, and when they leave, the Holiday Killer murders them all.

However, the best part of this issue came when Batman went into the sewers to find the criminals, and he ended up fighting Solomon Grundy, something he really didn't want to do. The end of the issue had a wonderful moment when a remorseful Batman returned to the sewers to leave Grundy a plate of food, allowing the monstrous man to enjoy a Thanksgiving dinner. This same scene was used in the two-part Batman animated film as well.

Even before Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars #1 (1984) by Jim Shooter and Michael Zeck came out, Marvel was already shaking up the entire brand in the most innovative way possible.

I was a kid when Marvel Comics prepared to start its Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars comic book series, and I had no idea what was going on. When Marvel Super Heroes Contest of Champions came out in 1982, it was incredible, especially for a kid who had never dreamed he would see so many heroes fighting in one series.

What Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars did was take that idea and blow up the entire Marvel Universe. First up, Spider-Man returned with a new black costume in The Amazing Spider-Man #252 , and Spider-Man could put it on and take it off by just commanding it with his mind. It took seven months before Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars #8 came out and showed him getting the costume, and it took even longer to prove this was a symbiote.
