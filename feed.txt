1998’s Lost in Space is an adaptation of the iconic 1960s television series of the same name, a series which was itself a loose adaptation of The Swiss Family Robinson just in space. It follows the Robinson family that embark on a journey to the stars, headed to a nearby star system to ready a new world for mass-emigration from an Earth that is rapidly becoming uninhabitable. While the 1960s series was campy and fun, the movie takes the concept and goes much more serious and a bit darker with it. The film preserves the idea of sabotage sending the Robinson’s off course, but there is something more sinister about things in the movie, and something a bit creepier about Doctor Zachary Smith (Oldman), the family physician who turns out to be a spy. The darkness is also present in the film’s overall visuals and aesthetic. That is something that was really cool about the 1990s in sci-fi, especially the late 1990s. It was visually darker and an interesting mix of both slick and gritty, and it was something that Lost in Space actually did very well. The film also had genuinely great practical effects and highly detailed sets. All of the actors do a solid job in the film, giving us characters who bounce off one another in ways that seems to fit their personalities.
