&nbsp; Share on X (Opens in new window) X Share on Facebook (Opens in new window) Facebook Add Us On Google An eminently rare Umbreon Pokémon TCG card just reached more than $200,000 at auction, adding another enormous six-figure result to an already extraordinary year for Pokémon card collectors. The 2013 Black & White Black Star Promo Umbreon #BW93, graded PSA 10 Gem Mint, commanded $216,000 with buyer's premium during Fanatics Collect's 253rd Weekly auction on November 22.

Although the Moonlight Pokémon has become accustomed to attracting astronomical prices, this particular result has an interesting wrinkle. Another PSA 10 example sold for $264,000 in September, meaning the latest sale actually fell $48,000 short of the existing record. Nevertheless, that's an extraordinary amount for a card originally distributed in a collection box, particularly considering a Gem Mint example fetched less than $10,000 just last year. With only 14 PSA 10 copies currently recorded, this Umbreon has developed into one of the more formidable (and unheralded) Eeveelution grails of the Black & White era. Let's take a gander at how it got there.

All-Time Record Sale: $264,000 in September 2026

Umbreon #BW93 may lack the widespread recognition of Evolving Skies’ famed Moonbreon Special Illustration Rare or the tremendous cachet of the iconic POP Series 5 Umbreon Gold Star, but the latest auction result demonstrates that collectors are willing to spend an outrageous amount of money for a pristine copy.

According to PSA's sales records , another Gem Mint copy commanded $264,000 through Fanatics Collect on September 28. The November result represents an 18.2% decline from that previous benchmark, which is hardly insignificant when dealing with such an expensive piece of cardboard. Still, $216,000 would have been an almost unimaginable sum for this particular promo just a few years ago. The card's 14-copy PSA 10 population certainly helps explain its appeal, especially as other Black & White-era Pokémon cards have recently commanded similarly staggering prices .

Beyond that, PSA's documented sales history includes a Gem Mint example that sold for just $9,900 on eBay in July 2025. Compared with that transaction, the latest $216,000 auction result represents an increase of $206,100, or approximately 2,082%, in less than a year and a half—nearly 22 times the previous price.

Intriguingly, the September record-setting copy was the very same card that sold for $9,900 in 2025, according to its PSA certification number. The market has clearly undergone a dramatic transformation in a remarkably short period, even if the latest result suggests that prices aren't necessarily destined to climb indefinitely. Given that six exceedingly rare Eeveelution cards combined for nearly $600,000 in an earlier auction this year, Umbreon's latest showing fits rather neatly into a broader pattern of extraordinary demand for Eevee's various evolutions.

Unlike the traditional booster-pack chase cards that dominate many high-end Pokémon auctions, Umbreon #BW93 originated in the Sylveon Collection, which was released on November 15, 2013. This collection box celebrated Sylveon's arrival in the TCG and included four Black & White booster packs, a Sylveon figurine, a Fairy Energy card, and nine promotional Pokémon cards featuring Eevee and all eight of its evolutions.

Umbreon was numbered BW93 and featured a distinctive Cosmos holofoil treatment and artwork credited to the entire The Pokémon Company Art Team (something only 15 other cards can boast). While this illustration had already appeared in Japan during the Pokémon Center's 2012 Eevee Collection campaign, its 2013 English release offered collectors a particularly attractive version of the fan-favorite Dark-type Pokémon, complete with its glowing yellow rings, elegantly mysterious aura, and moody coloration.

We've already witnessed three Southern Islands promos combine for $972,000 at auction , while Umbreon Gold Star's staggering $738,100 sale offers further evidence that collectors have an almost insatiable appetite for Eevee’s Dark-type evolution. Whether this particular Black & White promo eventually surpasses its $264,000 record remains to be seen, but one thing is crystal-clear: Umbreon is perfectly capable of turning a once-modest retail collectible into something worth more than a luxury sedan.

Suited up in their E-Frames, JT Marsh's Able Squad took the fight to the stars in large-scale battles that would impress the most discerning Star Wars fans. Unlike most of its contemporaries, Exosquad had one ongoing story where every episode mattered to the overall story arc of liberating Earth from the Neosapiens.

War is never fun, and Exosquad made sure its viewers understood the weight of what was happening amid its brightly colored mech battles and starships. It was fairly mature and as dark as it could be for its time, and would easily be one of the first candidates I would pick on this list for a modern gritty reboot since it would hardly need any changes to make it more adult. The show wrapped after two seasons, leaving its fans behind with a cliffhanger ending that would have introduced a new villain that would force the humans and Neosapiens to put aside their differences and work together to keep the peace in the galaxy. The biggest theme across this list will be these shows all ended too soon before they could truly end their series, and Exosquad will forever be one of my favorite shows that needed more time to finish its story.

I recently wrote about the life and times of this oddball anthropomorphic franchise , and briefly touched on its short-lived animated series, describing it as a cross between Star Wars and Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles . It was futuristic furry animals fighting space battles with laser pistols against a megalomaniacal military-industrial complex run by toads with plans for universal domination, so in some ways this was actually closer to Star Wars multiplied. Human Willy DeWitt helps kids watching at home have a character they can identify with, but he was barely a footnote in this crazy list of psychic cats, mercenary foxes, and sharpshooting waterfowl.

The crew of the Righteous Indignation crossed paths with bounty hunting purple alligators, frog-shaped mechs and Beetlegeusian Baboons, fighting the good fight like any interplanetary rebel alliance would against an evil empire of corporate stooges with the power to build an army. With a paltry 12 episodes under one season, Bu cky O'Hare and the Toad Wars is an easy watch if you can find it. It's a true shame this series never had more of a chance to complete its story across both animation and comic books, as there were clearly some places they wanted to go as far as storylines that never saw fruition. Don't be fooled by the cute animals and silly sounding concept. Bucky O'Hare is the real deal and he deserves some respect on his name. Plus, he has the best theme song of any cartoon on this list!

Superior to the majority of the cinematic sequels, Highlander: The Animated Series continued the tales of the MacLeod family and their quest for the Prize — but that's where the similarities between the movies and this cartoon end. Set centuries ahead of the rest of the franchise in a dystopian 27th century landscape, Quentin MacLeod learns that he is an immortal as well the last of his ancestry. With the tutelage of Ramirez (a different one from the Sean Connery version in the original movie) and help from his sister Clyde and Snarf-coded pet Gaul, the group travel the ruined world to battle and learn from the other immortals as they prepare for a showdown with the planet's dictator, Kortan.

A combination of fantasy and sci-fi wrapped into one, Highlander: The Animated Series was surprisingly violent for a show made for young kids. Even though they introduced a way to gain an immortal's powers without decapitating their foes, heads still rolled along with the on-screen deaths of many characters across the show's two seasons. Imagine if Fist of the North Star received a treatment by Disney, and you'd have a sense for how Highlander's swordfights played out — but even then you might still be surprised by how far they pushed Standards & Practices. The worldbuilding is top notch along with some good characters and storytelling that found a way to make an R-rated movie not only accessible to kids, but made something superb that honors the source material while creating something brand new.

Have you ever wondered what happens when you put a video game inside a console? Reboot answered that question with snazzy 1994 CGI graphics and self-contained episodes that used many classic video game genres, movies and TV shows to show off a unique virtual world unlike any that kids had seen on a Saturday morning before. It was like if Tron met Toy Story , but with a dose of The Matrix long before that existed. Every episode was a thrilling adventure, but there was also a larger story being played out that quietly shifted the tone from shenanigans into something more serious.

Reboot lasted for four seasons, with a three-year gap of production time between the third and final fourth season. Its last season revealed many secrets about the lore of the Mainframe, and ended on an unresolved cliffhanger that the shows creator still refuses to tell anyone. By then, things had become much darker and different from its first days on air, but this was a welcome change. The show remains a cult favorite, with many of its episodes viewable on Mainframe Studio's official YouTube channel. Reboot remains a very fun and often forgotten piece of 90's Saturday morning sci-fi

Nearly a decade after the original Transformers wrapped up their four season run, Beast Wars roared into action in September, 1996. The first series in the franchise to be fully animated using CGI (by the same folks behind Reboot ), Beast Wars removed the modern vehicles and placed the heroic Maximals and foul Predacons on prehistoric Earth, giving them organic forms instead of mechanical ones. The teams of warring factions were also significantly smaller than their predecessors, leaving more space for deeper character development and complex relationships that weren't so easy to do with a series that had dozens of characters vying for attention.

While Beast Wars had its share of light-hearted episodes (you could always rely on Waspinator for a laugh) or others focused purely on action, some delved into more serious waters like "Transmutate" and "Crossing the Rubicon." It also managed to connect the original 80's cartoon, proving that not even time and space can keep Starscream from being a nuisance to the good guys. After three excellent seasons, the show transitioned into "Beast Machines," which brought the heroes back to their home planet of Cybertron, but its one in which they lost and Megatron rules the world. This darker sequel series hit on many high sci-fi concepts, and is a must-watch companion if you plan on binging Beast Wars . Combined, they're a pair of unusual entries in the Transformers series, but that is what makes them such perfect parts of the franchise that should never be overlooked against its more mechanical-looking counterparts.
