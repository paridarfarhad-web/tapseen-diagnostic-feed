Pattinson benefited from utterly perfect timing. In other words, if you're betting the return of the Bond franchise on an actor, Pattinson's recent run had to be incredibly reassuring to the top brass at Amazon MGM. He has that combination of movie star appeal plus indie film credibility. Pattinson has also previewed playing a spy during his turn in Tenet and has proven he can lead a franchise across his time on Twilight and The Batman . Plus, director Denis Villeneuve worked with Pattinson on the next Dune , so the two already have a relationship.

That familiarity has a bit of a downside, however. Some are knocking the choice because Pattinson is already so well known from other films. The previous Bonds, by and large, were far less established as movie stars. &#8220;Pattinson is too popular&#8221; is a pretty weak knock, however.

Pattinson also brings something that the franchise desperately needs: A different vibe (&#8220;edgy&#8221; is a cheap description, but it fits).

Where Pattinson might come up short, for some, is that he's more interesting than he is traditionally handsome or sexy. The angular Pattinson often has a nervous or uncomfortable quality, which makes him highly watchable, but even his romantic roles tend toward quirkiness. When it came to fulfilling that part of the job description, Turner was probably the best fit.

But one thing Bond producers have struggled with in recent years is how to deal with the character's sexuality. How do you modernize a character who has so often been seen as a sexist, womanizing rogue while still preserving the franchise's escapist &#8220;men want to be him, women want to bed him&#8221; fantasy that's fundamentally a part of 007? Casting Pattinson might relieve some of the pressure to deliver a traditional Bond and suggest new tones and angles to play.

At 40, Pattinson is also notably older than the other actors who were under consideration. We'd been hearing for years that the franchise's producers were looking to refresh the franchise with a young Bond (oddly enough, Lowden, Turner, Josh O'Connor and Nicholas Hoult are all the same age &#x2014; 36). Pattinson is actually three years older than Daniel Craig when he was cast, so the whole &#8220;young Bond&#8221; idea went out the window (Henry Cavill fans are seething this morning, as the 43-year-old actor said in March that he would be considered too old for the part).

Craig brought a level of intense physicality and brute force the role that any actor would have a tough time matching. Still, Pattinson projects vulnerability as well, and checks so many other boxes &#x2014; a great British actor, a movie star, a box office draw, Gen Z appeal, handsome, a franchise veteran, and a team player (that last one, as we've previously written, is more important than you might think ).

While some of the other Bond candidates might have been more conventional choices, going conventional was a riskier move. You don't want a Bond who feels like he was grown in a lab to play Bond. You want an actor who can take the role somewhere new. When managing a 62-year-old franchise whose demise has been proclaimed countless times, Pattinson's the smartest, safest choice producers could have made &#x2014; a proven draw who can leave the character shaken and stirred.
