According to Puck News , talk of Rockstar bringing the license to Red Dead Redemption out into the world has started to create buzz in Hollywood. It's worth noting that the $100 million license price, which was noted as being the "minimum," is just the cost associated with a studio grabbing the rights to develop the property into a movie or TV show. That means that if a studio decided to bite and pay up, they're already down a nine-figure sum before they've even hired a writing team to bring the project to life. Since then, estimates have put the total amount Amazon has spent on The Rings of Power at over a billion dollars, when factoring in not only production and visual effects costs, but also marketing.

Considering the astronomical costs of a potential Red Dead Redemption adaptation, that's without even factoring in the uphill battle it would face with the player base of the game itself. Though the actual narrative of Red Dead Redemption games has captivated fans with how the story plays out, there's still an element of player choice that is at the heart of the titles that helped make them fan favorites. That said, video game adaptations have clearly entered a new era. TV shows like Fallout , The Last of Us , and Cyberpunk: Edgerunners have proven that they can work and create all-new fans who will then play the games themselves. In the end, that's the main reason for a game studio to license their title for another medium, something that even Take-Two admitted worked in their favor with the disaster of the Borderlands movie. Hopefully the same won't be said for Red Dead Redemption in a few years.

Pairing up toys and comics gives the opportunity to create a broader fictional universe around a pre-existing concept. Toys and comics were further intermingled in the 1980s — a great decade for both toys and comics — when several franchises included mini-comics with the release of individual toy figures. Now seen as valuable collectibles, these mini-comics were just bonuses with the toy when they first arrived. Some lines were more successful than others, launching line after line of mini-comics while others stopped after only three issues. Although toy giant Mattel acquired it in 1996, Tyco carved out its own niche in the toy market for a time. Tyco was founded in the 1950s as a producer of toy train kits before moving on to pre-built trains, slot cars, and remote control vehicles. In December of 1987, Tyco launched a toy line known as Dino-Riders. With each dinosaur figure released, kids got a mini-comic. Since it was a new toy, Tyco figured a comic would help market the action figures . According to the comic, Dino-Riders was about the battle between the superhuman Valorians and the humanoid Rulons, set during dinosaur times on Earth.

The comic was sixteen pages long, and Tyco would release two issues of it. The second issue continued the narrative from the first one and was included in each box of the toy’s second, third, and Ice-Age series. The mini-issues were written by regular Tyco freelancer Paul Kirchner. In addition to Dino-Riders , Kirchner wrote for Tyco’s Crash Dummies and Spy-Tech toy lines. It would hardly be his only contribution to toy comics, given his work with G.I. Joe, He-Man, Thundercats, and Power Rangers in the 1980s and 1990s.

Before the company filed for bankruptcy and had its products bought out by Hasbro in 1989, Coleco Industries, known for Cabbage Patch Dolls, created a line of action figures. Mantlo is perhaps better known for his work on the Micronauts and Rom , along with co-creating Rocket Raccoon and the pair Cloak and Dagger. While each toy’s mini-comic was meant to spotlight the character or playset it came with, some comics were more common than others, such as “The World of Symbion”.

Given that the line was new, the Masters of the Universe action figures came with a mini-comic meant to introduce not only the individual toy’s character but also the larger lore around Eternia.

While Super Powers started out as a toyline, it branched out into books, games, puzzles, toys, and was cross-branded with the Super Friends tv show.
