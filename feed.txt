What is The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping about?

The prequel story focuses on Haymitch&#x2019;s Hunger Games. Obviously since he was around to mentor Katniss and Peeta in their first and (unfortunately) second set of games, he was a victor of his own games, but that did not make them any more gruesome, for his games were the 50 th set.

Every 25 years, the capitol of Panem, the dystopian nation in which these stories take place, reinforces what is called the Quarter Quell, meaning a special wrinkle or rule is added to the Hunger Games before The Reaping ceremony, in which tribute names are drawn.

The wrinkle of Haymitch&#x2019;s games was that each district was required to send double the amount of tributes that year, so four competitors from each district doubled the amount of players in the games &#x2014; making it a whopping 48 versus the normal 24.

For those new to the universe entirely, each District of Panem &#x2014; there are 12 &#x2014; is forced to send two tributes each into the Hunger Games, a barbaric tradition that pits these pairs of usually children together in an arena to fight until the death until only one remains. Collins has spoken of the inspiration as gladiator fights and Greek mythology. The two tributes, a boy and a girl, are selected in a Reaping process which involves slips of paper with names on them in a bowl that a Capitol representative pulls from.

In District 12, specifically, where Katniss, Peeta, Haymitch AND Lucy Gray Baird are from, candidates can opt for extra slips of paper with their names on them to get more food or supplies, because the coal-mining district is a poor one.

In a l etter released right before the premiere and release of the film, Suzanne Collins recounted her experience coming up with Haymitch's story.

"The seeds of Haymitch Abernathy's backstory were planted over twenty years ago when I began the original trilogy," she wrote. "In Catching Fire , Katniss and Peeta watch the recap of his Second Quarter Quell games as he silently observes from the shadows. The Capitol had warped the true account and spun reality into vicious propaganda. For Haymitch to tell the truth about what happened would've been too dangerous for his tributes and too traumatic for him."

As the most adapted fictional work of all time, with almost 350 adaptations across movies, TV, stage, and more, there can surely be no purpose in retelling the story yet again. Yet despite the well-worn nature of the story, Johnny Depp is currently raking it in at the box office with his movie Ebenezer and I found this to be one of the best versions out there. As one of my favorite books, I have seen a lot of versions of Scrooge's redemption arc played out over the years to compare it to.

Ebenezer is Ti West's version of A Christmas Carol and it is perhaps therefore not surprising to hear that the film leans into some darker moments more than many of the previous adaptations. Along with Depp in the titular role, the cast is fleshed out with brilliant performances from Ian McKellen, Rupert Grint, Angela Riseborough, Tramell Tillman, and others and even brings a few little expansions of the story thanks to West's decision to show and not tell. This includes an opening prelude where Jacob Marley is not dead to begin with, and several other scenes that are described in the story by characters talking about them but are inserted here as full scenes. West also adds in his own extension to the story with an expanded roles for Scrooge's lost love, Belle, the murder of his one-time employer Fezziwig at the hands of a beggar, and even a glimpse into his troubled home-life as a child. That makes this the same story you know, but ever so slightly different at the same time.

When it was announced that Ti West would be directing Ebenezer it was hard to know exactly what audience he would be aiming it at. Having made his name with the X trilogy of horror movies, it would not have been surprising if the movie turned out to be a bloody R-rated affair. Instead, the film is much more than that. It is one of the darkest takes on the source material in terms of its focus on the more gothic elements of Victorian fiction, and being a ghost story at heart, this one does not hold back on delivering some creepy imagery . The ghosts of Jacob Marley, Past, Present, and Yet to Come are joined briefly by the unrestful dead, who beckon and urge Scrooge to save them in moments straight for the horror playbook, but that is only part of the overall tone of the movie.

West's movie sticks to Dickens' story to the point of actually using the novella's chapter titles on screen to break up the story into segments. The gloom and grimness of the era is almost palpable as the camera sweeps around the streets of London, showing off its grimy beggars, dirty windows, and hopeless society. Throw in some misty scenes, graveyards of haunted spirits, and a morbid moment of Ebenezer coming face to face with the Ghost of Christmas Yet to Come's true identity in one of several real moments of eerie horror. Although some movies have tinkered in this area, there have been very few that have really treat the story for what it is, and that is something that sets West's version apart from many of its peers.

Naturally, the make or break aspect of any adaptation of A Christmas Carol comes down to the man (or in some cases woman) playing the role of the miser turned good egg leading the movie. Over the years some of the greatest actors have embodied the role of Ebenezer Scrooge, from Michael Caine and Patrick Stewart to George C. Scott, Bill Murray in the contemporary Scrooged , and even Ryan Reynolds in Apple TV musical movie Spirited . Now, it is Johnny Depp's turn and he does not let the opportunity to fully embrace the role with all his heart. And just like some of the best actors, the aforementioned Caine among them, Depp is most at home when he is playing it as the same character he plays in many of his movies, and there is nothing wrong with this as it brings something to the role that no other actor can. He cackles and gibbers his way through some scenes, always with the greatest amount of expression on his prosthetic-covered face.

I have watched so many versions of A Christmas Carol that the story itself is one that brings no surprises. What that means is all it can do is use different visual tricks and slight nuances that can only be brought about by the vision of a director and the actors that bring the characters to life. Among the many versions told over the years, many will agree that the 1984 movie starring George C. Scott and 1992's The Muppet Christmas Carol are right up there as two of the best adaptations. Ebenezer cannot touch these, but for me it comes in not too far behind. What is interesting now is whether another new dark take on the story can come along and take its place very soon, with Robert Eggers set to follow up the success of Nosferatu and this year's upcoming Werewulf with his own Christmas Carol . Only time will tell on that score, but for now, Ti West and Johnny Depp's version is the most unique and memorable made this century.

Dwayne Johnson's portrayal of an amnesiac action star, the bizarre musical and dance numbers, and the inclusion of a rift in the space-time continuum all combine to make Southland Tales just a few steps beyond what general audiences are able to tolerate from a sci-fi movie. However, despite the film's lack of commercial success, it secured a loyal cult following who appreciate its uniquely bizarre mythology, and Southland Tales remains an underrated cult classic two decades after its release.

Despite technically being a sci-fi movie based on a Stephen King story , 1992's The Lawnmower Man took so many liberties with the source material that the famed author sued to have his name removed from all marketing. The film's story involves a scientist enhancing a man with developmental difficulties, using virtual reality and drugs to vastly increase his intellect. The film's blend of cyberpunk, techno-thriller, and psychological horror made for a pretty odd mix, especially as it was released at a time when audiences had little experience with even one of those sci-fi tropes, let alone all at once. The result was The Lawnmower Man being unable to secure any sort of widespread success, largely because both critics and audiences felt it was, all in all, a very weird movie. That didn't stop it from becoming a cult classic, though, with its early use of CGI technology and ambitious technological horror celebrated by its relatively small contingent of fans. Despite its many odd qualities, The Lawnmower Man remains a '90s cult classic, as well as an oft-forgotten chapter in sci-fi history.

The career of Tim Burton has seen the director deliver many hugely successful blockbusters, but Mars Attacks! isn't often named among them. The 1996 sci-fi movie is considered a cult classic , but it struggled to achieve mainstream success upon its initial release. The movie's central premise — aliens from Mars invading Earth — evokes timeless sci-fi ideas, but the execution was far from what many expected of Mars Attacks! . Approaching the whole premise as a B-movie realized with some of the best visual effects available at the time, and then offsetting its science fiction with slapstick comedy and deliberately cartoonish violence was, simply put, not what anyone expected. The air of deliberate tackiness proved off-putting to many, denying Mars Attacks! the mainstream success it really deserved. However, the movie quickly secured a cult following and has, in the decades since, been celebrated as an underrated sci-fi classic, outlining that just because a movie struggles to impress mainstream audiences, that doesn't mean it should be easily written off.

&nbsp; Share on X (Opens in new window) X Share on Facebook (Opens in new window) Facebook Add Us On Google Sidekicks have been an important part of adventures stories for millennia, so when DC Comics introduced Robin in 1940, it was just more fully embracing the mythological roots of the superhero. Since then, DC and Marvel Comics have created a variety of sidekicks, with some of the most popular ever often starting out as junior partners to the world's greatest heroes. Sidekicks are all about potential; their mentors train them so they can join the fight against evil, passing on what they've learned to the next generation. Often times, sidekicks are on the same power/skill level of their mentors, mostly because it makes it easier for them to train.

However, there have been times when heroes chose a sidekick who ended up outclassing them. These sidekicks sometimes had training before they started or tapped into greater sources of power as time went on. Other times, the heroes recruit a more powerful sidekick to help train them. While we all can name sidekicks that obviously outclass their mentors, there are others who don't look the part, which makes it all the more surprising. These five comic sidekicks are more powerful than the heroes training them, but you'd never know it by looking at them.

Bart Allen was introduced during Mark Waid's '90s run on The Flash . He was meant to be the new speedster sidekick, but the character made a pretty big splash right away, and was moved over to his own book. Bart is the grandson of Barry Allen from the 31st century (Barry went to the future after "The Trial of Barry Allen", where Iris was, and they started a family, birthing the Tornado Twins before Barry died in Crisis ), born with the Speed Force and raised in virtual reality. He was the ultimate ADHD kid.

Bart wasn't Kid Flash, though , at least not back then. He wasn't the Flash's sidekick. Instead, he was being trained by two of the oldest speedsters on the planet – Jay Garrick, who he was living with in Keystone City, and Max Mercury, the Zen speedster who had posited the existence of the Speed Force years before it was discovered. While Jay and Max are both potent heroes, they're more skilled than powerful. Bart's connection to the Speed Force was much more powerful than theirs, allowing him to outpace his mentors when he really turned on the power.

Damian Wayne is the son of Batman and Talia al Ghul, the heir to the Demon's Head and the mantle of the Dark Knight. He was introduced in the classic "Batman and Son", a story that seemingly ended with his death. However, it was only the beginning for young Damian. He'd stay in the shadows until his father's "death" in Final Crisis , when he tried to step up as the heir of the Bat. However, he was 12, which meant that he was more suited for another Bat-mantle – that of Robin. Damian ended up becoming the Boy Wonder when Dick Grayson took over as Batman, with the two of them forming a different Dynamic Duo.

Damian was trained almost from birth by his mother to be better than both his grandfather and father. I think saying that Damian is better than Bruce might be a stretch – although he probably has the potential to be – however, he was more skilled than his first mentor, Dick. Grayson is one of the best superheroes out there, but Damian was his superior in numerous ways. Dick knew it too, and treated Damian with respect, helping him become a a better person and hero.

Once upon a time, Aquaman needed a sidekick. Instead of calling him Aquaboy, which isn't great but not that bad, they decided to go another direction and Aqualad was born. That name was terrible – made worse by the fact that his real name was Garth – and he'd become one of the least popular DC sidekicks of them all. He couldn't even get onto the New Teen Titans and he was one of the founding members of the original group. The death of Aquagirl during Crisis left him adrift and he faded away with the rest of the Aquaman mythos in the late '80s. However, Peter David's brilliant Aquaman (Vol. 4) salvaged the character , with Garth getting a chance to reach his highest potential.

Atlantis was once the home of humanity's magic back before it sank, so Atlanteans mages are especially powerful. It was eventually revealed that Garth was a powerful magic user and he ended up becoming Tempest (go look up his red and black costume at the time – he looked amazing). While Arthur's aqua-telepathy and superhuman physicality made him powerful, Tempest's magic gave him the greater potential. Of course, since Arthur has connected to the Blue, he's certainly more powerful than Garth, but there was a long time that Tempest was more powerful than Aquaman.

Cassandra Cain is the daughter of two of the greatest assassins of them all, David Cain and Lady Shiva. She was trained to enter the family business and Cain went to some extremes to make his daughter deadly. He wouldn't speak at all around her, forcing her to learn to read body language to figure out what people wanted or were going to do next. This made her amazing martial artist, allowing her to predict her foes' move and use the perfect counter against them. She didn't want to be a killer though, and ended up escaping to Gotham City, which had been ravaged by disease and an earthquake.

During this time, Huntress was dressing as Batman, creating rumors of a new Batgirl. Cass would end up taking up the costume after Huntress dropped it and joined the Bat-Family. Cass was trained by Barbara Gordon to be a hero, but everyone knew that she could waste Barbara even if she wasn't in the wheelchair at that point (and that's saying something, because Barbara as Oracle was known for destroying everyone who thought she was a pushover because she was paralyzed). Even Batman caught those hands. Well, "caught" is the wrong thing because it implies that he could do anything about it, which he can't. Cass is a better fighter even than the Dark Knight, making her one of the most potent martial artists of them all.

Spider-Man is Marvel's most relatable hero and quickly became one of the most popular superheroes ever. However, Spidey was a teen when he started, so he was a bit too young for a sidekick. Over the years, the Wall-Crawler would get partners, heroes who fought alongside him, but rarely ever had what would be considered his own sidekick. In the 2000s, he ended up joining the Avengers and becoming a more important hero than ever, which would eventually lead to him getting a sidekick, the teen known as Alpha.

Andy Maguire was at Horizon Labs and ended up in a lab accident that was similar to Peter's. However, instead of getting spider powers or something like that, he ended up gaining a measure of control over cosmic energies. Andy became Alpha and Spider-Man ended up working with the young hero, showing him the ropes. However, Alpha was never very popular with readers and has been fighting crime in Pittsburgh, far away from the center of the Marvel Universe.
